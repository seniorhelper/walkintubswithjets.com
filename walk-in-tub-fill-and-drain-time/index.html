How long does a walk-in tub take to fill and drain?

Short answer: filling usually takes about 5 to 10 minutes, depending on your faucet and water pressure. Draining takes about 6 to 15 minutes for a standard drain, or as little as 2 to 4 minutes with a fast-drain system. Because you sit inside with the door closed for both, a 20-minute soak can mean 30 to 45 minutes in the tub. The calculator below shows your own timeline.

Reviewed October 2026. Ranges are from Retirement Living, Safe Step and published spec sheets, listed under Sources.

Why fill and drain time matter more in a walk-in tub

With a regular tub you can fill it, then step in. A walk-in tub works the other way: you sit down, close the watertight door, and only then fill. The door cannot open until the water is gone, because the weight of the water is what helps hold it shut. So you are seated for the whole fill and the whole drain.

For someone who gets chilly, that wait is the most common complaint. A jetted tub adds one more wrinkle: water jets should not run until the water is above the highest jet, so you cannot start the massage early.

What makes filling slow

  • The faucet. Some walk-in tubs offer high-flow faucets. Retirement Living mentions models rated up to 14 gallons per minute that can fill a standard tub in about four minutes.
  • Your home’s water pressure. Low pressure means a slow fill no matter which faucet you buy. Your bucket test shows what your house can really deliver.
  • Tub size. One Ella’s Bubbles spec sheet for a 90-gallon tub lists about 14 minutes to fill, based on household water pressure. Bigger tubs and two-seat models take longer.
  • Running out of hot water. If your water heater cannot supply enough hot water, the last part of the fill turns lukewarm. An inline heater keeps warm water warm; it cannot fix a cold fill. Our sister site explains how to check your water heater.

What makes draining slow

  • The drain design. Safe Step puts the average walk-in tub at 6 to 15 minutes to drain. Fast-drain systems with larger or dual drains, or a pump assist, can cut that to a few minutes. The same Ella spec sheet lists about 2 minutes to drain under ideal plumbing conditions.
  • Your home’s drain line. An older, narrow, or partly clogged line limits even the best tub drain.
  • Hair and soap. A partly blocked stopper adds minutes. Clean it often.

Six ways to stay warm and comfortable during the wait

  1. Warm the room first. Run the bathroom heater or close the door a few minutes before your bath.
  2. Drape a towel over your shoulders while the tub fills, and keep a robe within reach for the drain.
  3. Look for a heated seat or backrest if you are shopping. Ask which models have one.
  4. Start the air jets early if your manual allows it; some air systems warm the air they push.
  5. Use a handheld shower on your shoulders while the water drains.
  6. Ask about fast-drain options before you buy.

Safety: never open the door to hurry the drain. Water will flood the floor and can make you slip. If you feel unwell in the tub, use the drain, call for help, and wait for the water to go down.

Questions people ask

Can I open the walk-in tub door before it drains?

No. The water presses on the door and seal, and opening it would flood the bathroom. Stay seated until the tub is empty.

Is the "fill time" on a spec sheet accurate?

Treat it as a best case. Makers test with good water pressure and an empty tub. Your bucket test is a better guide to your home.

Does a fast drain need special plumbing?

Some fast-drain systems rely on a larger drain line or a pump. Ask the installer to look at your existing drain before you choose one; an older or narrow line may limit how fast any drain can work.

How many gallons does a walk-in tub hold?

It varies widely by model, from compact tubs to large two-seat models. Ella’s Bubbles lists one model at 90 gallons. Check the spec sheet for the model you are quoted, and remember that water jets need the level above the top jet.

Can I fill the tub before getting in?

No. With a walk-in tub you sit first, close the door, then fill. That is the trade-off for the low step-in.
