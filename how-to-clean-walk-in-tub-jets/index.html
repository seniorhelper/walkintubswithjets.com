Care and sanitizing

How to clean walk-in tub jets, step by step

Direct answer: for water (whirlpool) jets, fill the tub with warm water until it is about 2 inches above the highest jet, add the cleaner your manual approves (often a low-foam dishwasher detergent, sometimes with household bleach), run the jets 5–15 minutes, drain, then refill with plain water and run again to rinse. Do it once or twice a month. For air jets, wipe the small holes and let the automatic purge cycle finish after each bath. Always check your manual first, because some makers rule out bleach.

Why jets need cleaning at all

A water-jet system recirculates bath water through a pipe loop behind the tub wall. When you drain the tub, a little water and soap film stay in that loop. Over days, that film can grow into biofilm, a sticky layer of microbes that clings to pipe walls and resists light cleaning.

This is not just a theory. In news coverage from around 2006, Texas A&M microbiologist Dr. Rita Moyes reported finding bacterial growth in every whirlpool bath she sampled, and she blamed water trapped in the pipes that gets pushed into the tub when the jets switch on. Her work looked at whirlpool baths in general, not walk-in tubs in particular, but the lesson carries over: a regular flush is part of owning water jets.

MedlinePlus, from the National Library of Medicine, explains that the germ behind hot tub folliculitis, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, survives in hot tub water, and that the rash can come back if the tub is used again before it has been cleaned. A walk-in tub is drained after every bath, which helps a great deal, but the pump loop is the one place old water lingers.

Air jets are different. The channels carry air, and check valves help keep bath water out. Many air systems also run a purge cycle that blows out any water that does get in.

How to flush water (whirlpool) jets

Before you start: read the care page in your owner’s manual. Some manufacturers do not recommend bleach because it can dry out internal gaskets over time; white vinegar is a common gentler choice. Never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia. Open a window or run the fan.

  1. Close off the air controls. If your jets have air-intake dials, turn them off so the cleaner circulates through the water lines instead of foaming. Kohler’s walk-in bath guide gives this as the first step.
  2. Fill with warm water above the jets. Fill until the water is at least 2 inches above the highest jet. Stay seated outside the tub; you do not need to be in it.
  3. Add the approved cleaner. Kohler suggests low-foam powdered dishwasher detergent with household bleach. Safe Step’s care advice uses about a cup of regular bleach (not the “splashless” kind) and a tablespoon of automatic dishwasher powder. A dedicated jetted-tub cleaner from a hardware store also works if your manual allows it.
  4. Run the jets. Kohler says 5–10 minutes; Safe Step suggests about 15. Watch for any dark specks coming out; that is the residue you are removing.
  5. Drain completely.
  6. Rinse cycle. Refill with plain warm water above the jets, run them again for 5–10 minutes, and drain.
  7. Wipe the surfaces. Use a soft cloth and a non-abrasive, ammonia-free cleaner on the shell. Rough pads scratch acrylic and gelcoat, and scratches hold grime.

What one maker’s manual adds

Ella’s Bubbles, whose tubs we sell, gives three specific instructions in its owner’s manual (rev. 03/18):

  • Flush once a month. That is the baseline; heavier use calls for more.
  • Clean the pump inlet cover monthly. Unscrew the suction cover low in the tub (counterclockwise), clear hair and debris from the cover and its housing, and screw it back on. Never run the jets with the cover off: the pump pulls hard enough to cause injury or damage.
  • Use acrylic-safe cleaners only. The manual recommends white vinegar and water and lists products to avoid, including anything with ammonia, abrasive powders and pads, and melamine sponges such as Magic Eraser. Using them can void the warranty.

Ella’s water-jet packages also include an ozone unit in the pump loop. It helps keep the water side cleaner but does not replace the monthly flush.

How to care for air jets

  1. Let the purge finish. Many air systems run an automatic purge a while after you stop the jets. One manual describes a 1–3 minute purge that begins about 15–20 minutes after the air jets turn off, and warns not to press the air button while it runs.
  2. Wipe the holes. Once a week, wipe the air holes with a soft cloth and mild soap. A soft toothbrush clears soap film from small holes.
  3. Run a monthly dry purge if your tub has one. Some models have a manual dry or purge button. Use it as the manual says.
  4. Keep products to what the manual allows. Air systems often tolerate salts better than water jets, but heavy oils can still leave film.

Do not forget the door seal and drain

The door seal is what keeps your bathroom floor dry. Wipe it after baths and look it over weekly for cracks, flattening or trapped hair. Safe Step’s care advice mentions wiping the seal with rubbing alcohol. Clear hair from the drain stopper often; a slow drain means a longer wait in a cooling tub. Questions people ask

How often should I clean walk-in tub jets?

For water jets, the advice we found ranges from monthly (Safe Step) to at least twice a month (Kohler). If you bathe often or share the tub, lean toward twice a month. Air jets mostly need the holes wiped and the purge cycle allowed to run.

Can I use vinegar instead of bleach?

Many people do, and some manufacturers prefer it because bleach can dry out gaskets over time. Add white vinegar to the flush water instead of bleach and detergent, run the jets, drain and rinse. Your manual or dealer can tell you how much suits your tub. Never combine vinegar and bleach in the same flush.

Do I need to climb into the tub to clean it?

No. You can do every step from a chair beside the open door before you fill, then close the door, fill and run the jets from outside. If bending is hard, a long-handled soft sponge helps with the shell.

Are air jets truly self-cleaning?

Not entirely. The purge cycle clears water from the lines, but soap film can still build up on the holes. A weekly wipe keeps them working.

What if I have not cleaned the jets in a long time?

Run two full cleaning flushes back to back, then a plain-water rinse. If flakes keep coming after that, call your dealer; a stubborn buildup may need a stronger, system-specific cleaner.
