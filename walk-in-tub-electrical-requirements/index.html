Wiring and safety

Walk-in tub electrical requirements, explained for homeowners

Direct answer: a jetted walk-in tub runs on ordinary 120-volt power, but the National Electrical Code (NEC 680.71) puts the tub and its equipment on an individual branch circuit protected by a readily accessible GFCI. Add up the amps on the spec sheet: an Ella’s Bubbles Deluxe with water jets, air jets and the inline heater draws about 18 amps with everything on, more than one 20-amp circuit should carry, which is why most combination tubs need two or three dedicated GFCI circuits. A licensed electrician and your local permit office have the final word.

Reviewed October 15, 2026. Code sections are from NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code; states and cities adopt editions on their own schedules. This page explains requirements in plain words; it is not a wiring guide.

What the electrical code requires, section by section

The code treats a jetted tub that is filled and drained after every use as a hydromassage bathtub, and it has its own short part of Article 680 (Part VII). That is different from a hot tub or spa, which keeps its water and falls under other parts of the same article. Here is what each section means for a walk-in tub:

Code sectionWhat it says, in plain wordsWhat it means at your house
NEC 680.70Hydromassage bathtubs follow Part VII of Article 680, not the pool and spa rules.Your electrician works from a short, specific list rather than hot-tub rules.
NEC 680.71The tub and its electrical components go on an individual branch circuit, protected by a readily accessible ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI).A new circuit from the panel that serves nothing else, and a GFCI you can reach to test and reset without moving the tub.
NEC 680.73The tub’s electrical equipment must be reachable without damaging the building structure or finish.Keep the front apron or access panel removable. Do not tile over it.
NEC 680.74Metal parts in contact with the circulating water, the pump and blower motors, and nearby metal piping are bonded together.A bonding wire ties the metal parts together so they stay at the same voltage. Double-insulated motors are an exception.
NEC 210.8(A)Receptacles in a dwelling’s bathroom need GFCI protection.Every outlet in the bathroom, not just the tub’s, should be GFCI protected.
NEC 430.22Conductors for a single motor are sized at 125% of its full-load current.Put another way, keep a motor at or below about 80% of its circuit. That is the 80% line in the checker below.

Why so strict? Water, people and motors share a small space. A GFCI watches for current leaking out of the circuit, for example through water, and cuts power in a fraction of a second. A regular breaker only protects the wires from overload; it does nothing for a person in the tub.

Each state and city adopts a code edition on its own schedule and can add local amendments. Section numbers above follow recent editions; your electrician and permit office decide what applies to your home.

What manufacturer manuals ask for

The code sets the floor. The manual for your model can ask for more, and it is the document the inspector and the warranty rely on. Here is what published documents say:

SourceWhat it asks for
Ella’s Bubbles owner’s manual (rev. 03/18)A GFCI-protected circuit installed and tested by a licensed electrician, no extension cords, electrical needs that vary by jet package
Ella’s Bubbles Deluxe spec sheetDedicated GFCI-protected 110/120 V, 60 Hz line(s): water pump 7 A, air pump 4.5 A, inline heater 6 A, LED light 0.5 A
Ella’s Bubbles Companion and Escape sheetsTwo 7 A water pumps, a 4.5 A air pump and a 5.5 A heater; dedicated GFCI-protected line(s)
A Hydro Systems install guideDedicated 20-amp GFCI for the pump, 15-amp for the blower and 15-amp for the heater
A Costco-sold walk-in tub manualOne dedicated 20-amp GFCI-protected circuit

The pattern: plan on at least one new dedicated GFCI circuit, and expect two or three for a combination tub with a heater or a two-pump tub. An air-only tub with no heater is usually the simplest.

A detail worth knowing: spec sheets sometimes list watts that do not match amps × 120 volts. The Deluxe lists its water pump at 7 A and 1,000 W, but 7 × 120 is 840. Use the amps for circuit planning and the watts for running cost.

Testing the GFCI at home

Ella’s manual asks owners to test the GFCI periodically: press TEST, and the device should trip and cut power; press RESET to restore it. If it does not trip, the manual says to turn the power off and not use the tub until the problem is found. Once a month is an easy habit to remember.

If you are replacing an old tub

A plain bathtub has no wiring, so swapping one for a jetted walk-in tub almost always means new circuits. That is part of why a one-day installation still involves a licensed electrician. Questions people ask

Can a walk-in tub plug into a regular outlet?

Some models come with a cord and plug, but the outlet still has to be on the tub’s own GFCI-protected branch circuit, and it has to be reachable through the access panel. A shared bathroom outlet is not the same thing, and Ella’s manual rules out extension cords.

Does a walk-in tub need 240 volts?

The Ella’s Bubbles sheets we checked all list 110/120-volt equipment. Some other models or large heaters differ, so read the voltage line for each component on your spec sheet.

Why does my tub need more than one circuit?

Pumps, blower and heater can all run at once. If their amps add up to more than about 80% of one circuit, or the maker simply asks for separation, each gets its own breaker. An Ella Deluxe adds up to about 18 amps, which is over the 16-amp line for a 20-amp circuit.

Is the GFCI the same as a regular breaker?

No. A regular breaker protects the wires from overload. A GFCI protects people by cutting power when it senses current leaking, for example through water. NEC 680.71 requires GFCI protection for hydromassage tubs, and many electricians use a GFCI breaker to do it.

Can I do the wiring myself?

Manuals say a licensed electrician should do it, and many towns require a permit and inspection. Incorrect wiring near water is dangerous and can void the warranty.
