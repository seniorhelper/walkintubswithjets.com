Jet systems explained

Air jets vs water jets: which belongs in your walk-in tub?

Direct answer: pick water jets (often called whirlpool or hydro jets) if you want firm pressure aimed at a sore back, hips or calves. Pick air jets if you want a soft, all-over fizz, have fragile skin, or like Epsom salt and bath oils. Pick a combination system if two people with different tastes will share the tub, or you honestly cannot decide. Neither type is “better”; they do different jobs.

How each system works, in plain words

Water jets

A pump pulls bath water in through a suction cover, pushes it through a loop of pipe behind the tub wall, and shoots it back out through a handful of nozzles. You feel a steady stream on one spot. Many nozzles swivel, and some can be turned down or shut.

Air jets

A blower pushes warmed air through channels under the tub floor and seat. The air escapes through many small holes and rises as bubbles all around you. Bath water itself does not travel through the air channels, which matters for cleaning.

Combination

Both systems in one shell, each with its own button. You can run water jets for your lower back, then switch to air for a gentle finish, or run both at once.

One spec sheet shows how a maker splits the jobs. The Ella’s Bubbles Deluxe lists 14 water jets (8 in the seat, 6 at the feet) on a 7-amp, 1,000-watt pump, and 16 air jets (8 in the seat, 8 at the feet) on a 4.5-amp, 500-watt three-speed blower that dries its own lines 20 minutes after you switch it off. The water side also carries an ozone unit and an intensity dial that mixes air into each stream. Two-seat models such as the Escape and Companion go further and give the seat jets and the foot-and-calf jets a pump each.

Where microbubble fits

Some tubs offer a third system, sold as microbubble therapy. It is not a massage. A separate pump pushes water through a pressure vessel, where air dissolves into it, and an agitator valve releases it as a cloud of very fine bubbles that turns the water milky. Ella lists it as a 1 HP pump drawing 7.3 amps (875 W on the Companion sheet).

Two practical points follow from those numbers. It is a third motor, so it adds to the electrical plan, and its draw is in the same range as a water-jet pump. And because it is about the feel of the water rather than pressure, it does not replace water jets for a sore back. Claims about skin benefits come mainly from the makers; we did not find controlled trials of home microbubble baths, which our research summary explains.

What living with each one is like

Water jets: strong, but they keep a little bath water

The pump loop cannot drain completely, so a small amount of old water sits in the pipe between baths. That is why makers ask you to run a cleaning flush on a schedule. Kohler’s walk-in bath guide suggests cleaning whirlpool jets at least twice a month. The payoff is real pressure: a nozzle aimed at the small of your back feels nothing like bubbles.

The water level also has to sit above the highest jet before the pump runs. Ella’s manual is blunt about it: running the pumps before the jets are covered can permanently damage the equipment. So a water-jet tub can need a fuller fill than you might use for a plain soak. Our fill and drain calculator helps you see what that means for your wait time.

Air jets: softer, and kinder to bath salts

Because the channels carry air, not bath water, many air systems run an automatic purge a while after you finish. Ella’s spec sheets put it at 20 minutes after the blower stops; the controller section of its owner’s manual describes a 2-minute blower run 15 minutes after the systems shut off or the tub drains. That blows out any water that seeped in. It is one reason people who love Epsom salt lean toward air.

The trade-off: air bubbles will not dig into a knot the way a water jet does. If your main goal is easing one stubborn sore spot, air alone may leave you wanting more.

Combination: the hedge

Combination tubs carry two motors, two sets of lines and more buttons, so there is more to clean and more to power if you run both at once. Ask for a written price on each version before deciding. The benefit is flexibility, and for couples it can settle the argument.

A note on health. Neither system is right for everyone. Heart trouble, blood pressure that drops when you stand, numb feet, open wounds and drowsy medicines are the usual reasons to check with a doctor first, and the manuals say so too. Our page on who should not use jets goes through each one with its source, and the jets and arthritis guide covers settings and water temperature.

Questions people ask

Are whirlpool jets and water jets the same thing?

Yes. Sellers use “whirlpool,” “hydro” and “water jets” for the same idea: a pump recirculates bath water through nozzles. “Air bath,” “air massage” and “air jets” all mean a blower pushing air through small holes.

Can I use Epsom salt in a walk-in tub with jets?

It depends on the system and the maker. Most water-jet manuals warn against salts, oils and bubble bath because they foam or leave film in the pump loop. Many air-jet systems are more forgiving. Read the care section for your model, because using a banned product can affect the warranty.

Which is quieter, air or water jets?

They sound different rather than one being silent. A water pump makes a steady hum; an air blower makes a louder rushing sound. If noise bothers you, ask to hear the tub running in a showroom or ask the dealer for a video.

Do air jets cool the water down?

Blowing room air through bath water can take heat away. Some air systems warm the air first to reduce this; the spec sheet will say. An inline heater, if your tub has one, helps hold temperature during the soak, but it is meant to keep warm water warm, not heat cold water.

Can I add jets to a walk-in tub later?

In practice, no. Jet plumbing, air channels and motors are built into the shell at the factory. Decide before you order.

Is a combination system worth it for one person?

Only if you truly want both feelings. If your answers in the chooser above leaned clearly one way, a single system keeps cleaning and power draw simpler.
