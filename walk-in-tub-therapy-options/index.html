What therapy options can a walk-in tub have?

Every walk-in tub starts as a soaker: a door, a seat and a deep bowl. Everything else is an option that adds a motor, a heater or a light, and each one adds amps to your wiring, a few cents to each bath and something to learn on the control pad. The figures below come from the Ella’s Bubbles Companion and Escape spec sheets and the Ella owner’s manual. Other models differ, so always read the sheet for the exact tub you are quoted.

On the research side, the warmth has more support than the jets do. Microbubble systems are the newest option. The skin claims come mainly from the makers, and we found no independent controlled trials of home microbubble baths. If you are curious anyway, the Ella’s Bubbles microbubble walk-in tub page lists the sizes it comes in.

How many circuits will my package need?

Under NEC 680.71, a hydromassage tub needs an individual branch circuit with a readily accessible GFCI. Electricians also keep the load a motor or heater runs for a long time under about 80% of the breaker, which is 16 amps on a 20-amp circuit. A single water pump (7 A) and heater (5.5 A) fit that line; add an air blower (4.5 A) and the total of 17 A does not. That is why combination tubs often need two or three dedicated circuits, and why the builder above shows a count. Ella’s heated-seat controller is a separate small load: its manual shows it plugging into a 120-volt, 15-amp GFCI supply. Our electrical requirements guide walks through the code sections and has an electrician checklist. A licensed electrician makes the final call, and many towns require a permit.

Will the VA pay for jets?

The VA’s Home Improvements and Structural Alterations grant (HISA) pays up to $6,800 or $2,000 over a veteran’s lifetime, depending on the disability, for changes that give access to essential bathroom facilities. Its official page lists “Spa, hot tub, or Jacuzzi” among the things HISA will not pay for, so a jetted package is unlikely to qualify. The main site’s HISA grant guide covers the application steps.

Questions people ask

Which walk-in tub option matters most for comfort?

For most bathers it is warmth: a heated seat and backrest for the minutes you sit while the tub fills and drains, and an inline heater to hold the water temperature while the jets run. Jets are a massage feature on top of that. Our staying-warm guide explains both.

Can I add jets or a heater to a walk-in tub later?

Plan on choosing them when you order. Pumps, blowers, heaters and jet plumbing are built into the shell at the factory, and the Ella’s Bubbles spec sheets list microbubble and the heated seat and backrest as optional features picked with the tub.

How much electricity do the options use?

On Ella’s Bubbles spec sheets a water-jet pump is 1,000 watts, the air blower 500 watts, the inline heater 650 watts and the microbubble pump 875 watts. Run for 15 minutes each at the July 2026 U.S. average of 18.31 cents per kWh, that is about 2 to 5 cents per option per bath. Heating the bath water itself costs far more.

Does chromotherapy lighting do anything?

It is a multicolor LED light. It can make the tub easier to see into in a dim bathroom and many people find it relaxing, but we found no controlled trials of colored tub lights as a health treatment, so treat it as a comfort extra.

Will the VA HISA grant pay for a jetted walk-in tub?

Probably not for the jets. The VA’s HISA page lists “Spa, hot tub, or Jacuzzi” among things HISA will not pay for. A roll-in shower is the example VA gives for bathroom access. Ask your VA Prosthetics office before you order.
